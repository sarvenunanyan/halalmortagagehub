Halal Mortgage Calculator: How Much Can You Borrow in 2025?

7 min read · Updated May 2025 · By the Halal Mortgage Hub team

One of the first questions anyone buying a home asks is: how much can I borrow? For halal mortgages, the answer depends on the same core factors as conventional mortgages — your income, outgoings, deposit, and credit history — but the specific calculations can vary by provider.

This guide explains exactly how halal mortgage affordability is assessed, with real examples to help you estimate your borrowing potential before approaching a lender.

Quick estimate: Most halal mortgage providers will lend between 4x and 5x your annual income, subject to affordability checks and credit assessment. A household income of £60,000 could typically support a finance amount of £240,000–£300,000.

How Halal Mortgage Affordability Is Calculated

Islamic mortgage providers use two main methods to assess how much you can borrow:

1. Income Multiple

The simplest approach: your income is multiplied by a set figure to arrive at a maximum finance amount. Most providers use a multiple of 4x to 5x for single applicants, and 4x to 4.5x for joint applicants based on combined income.

2. Affordability Assessment

This is more detailed — and increasingly the primary method used. The provider looks at your actual income and expenditure to determine what monthly payment you can genuinely afford. They'll stress-test this against potential increases in profit rates to ensure you could still afford payments in a higher-rate environment.

Factors That Affect Your Borrowing Amount

Deposit Size

A larger deposit directly increases your borrowing power in two ways: it reduces the finance amount needed, and it typically unlocks better profit rates. Most providers have tiered pricing, with the best rates available at 40%+ deposit.

Existing Financial Commitments

Monthly commitments — car finance, personal loans, credit cards, student loans — reduce the amount providers are willing to lend. Paying down existing debts before applying can meaningfully increase your maximum finance amount.

Number of Dependants

Providers factor in the cost of dependants (children, elderly relatives you support financially) when assessing affordability. More dependants generally means a lower maximum borrowing amount.

Employment Type

Employed applicants with a consistent salary typically qualify for the highest income multiples. Self-employed applicants, contractors, and those with variable income may face more conservative assessments or be asked to demonstrate a longer track record of earnings.

Credit History

A clean credit history unlocks the best rates and highest multiples. Missed payments, defaults, or CCJs will reduce both your maximum borrowing amount and the profit rates available to you.

Tip: Use a halal mortgage broker to find the provider whose criteria best match your specific circumstances. Some providers are more flexible with self-employed income; others offer higher multiples for professionals in certain fields.

How to Maximise Your Borrowing Amount

  • Pay down existing debts before applying — especially credit cards and personal loans
  • Increase your deposit if possible — even moving from 15% to 20% can unlock better rates and terms
  • Apply jointly if you have a partner — combined income typically supports a significantly higher finance amount
  • Improve your credit score — a higher score means more providers will consider you and better rates will be available
  • Avoid new credit applications in the 6 months before your mortgage application
  • Get a Decision in Principle early — this confirms your borrowing capacity before you start property searching

Income Types Accepted

Most halal mortgage providers accept the following income types in their affordability calculations:

  • Employment salary (PAYE)
  • Self-employment income (with 2–3 years' accounts)
  • Rental income (typically at 75–80% of actual rent received)
  • Investment income (dividends, interest from Sharia-compliant investments)
  • Pension income
  • Child benefit and maintenance payments (some providers)

Note: Benefits income and zero-hours contract income are assessed differently by each provider — always check directly with your chosen lender.
