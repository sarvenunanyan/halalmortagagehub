How to Apply for a Halal Mortgage: Step-by-Step Guide 2025

8 min read · Updated May 2025 · By the Halal Mortgage Hub team

Applying for a halal mortgage follows a similar process to a conventional mortgage — but there are important differences in how the product is structured, what documents you need, and what questions the provider will ask. This step-by-step guide walks you through everything from initial preparation to completion.

Good news: The halal mortgage application process has become significantly more streamlined in 2025. Most major providers now offer online applications, and many decisions are made within 24–48 hours.

Before You Apply: Getting Mortgage Ready

Preparation is the most important part of any mortgage application. Taking time to get your finances in order before you apply significantly increases your chances of approval — and often leads to better profit rates.

Tip: Check your credit report at least 3 months before applying. Errors on your credit file are surprisingly common and can take weeks to correct.

Step-by-Step Application Process

01

Assess Your Financial Position

Calculate your income, existing commitments, and how much deposit you have. Most halal mortgage providers require a minimum 10–20% deposit, though larger deposits unlock better profit rates. Use a mortgage calculator to get a realistic estimate of what you can afford before approaching any provider.

02

Check and Improve Your Credit Score

Islamic mortgage providers use the same credit scoring systems as conventional lenders. Check your score with Experian, Equifax, or TransUnion (UK) or Experian, Equifax, or TransUnion (US). Address any issues — late payments, errors, or high credit utilisation — well before applying.

03

Research and Compare Providers

Not all halal mortgage providers offer the same products or serve the same regions. In the UK, major providers include Al Rayan Bank, Gatehouse Bank, and Ahli United Bank. In the US, Guidance Residential, UIF, and Ameen Housing are leading options. Compare profit rates, fees, and product terms carefully.

04

Get a Decision in Principle (DIP)

Also called an Agreement in Principle or Mortgage in Principle, this is a conditional offer from a provider stating how much they're willing to lend. It doesn't affect your credit score significantly (soft search) and gives you confidence when making offers on properties. Most providers issue a DIP within 24 hours.

05

Gather Your Documents

Once you have a property in mind, you'll need to submit a full application with supporting documents. See the complete document checklist below.

06

Submit Your Full Application

Submit your application with all required documents. The provider will conduct a full credit check (hard search), verify your income, and assess the property through a valuation survey. This process typically takes 2–6 weeks depending on the provider and complexity of your case.

07

Property Valuation

Your provider will commission a valuation of the property. For Diminishing Musharaka structures, this is particularly important as the provider is co-purchasing the property with you. The valuation confirms the property is worth the purchase price and is suitable as security.

08

Receive Your Formal Offer

If your application is approved and the valuation is satisfactory, you'll receive a formal mortgage offer. Review this carefully with a solicitor. For Islamic mortgages, the legal documentation will reflect the Sharia-compliant structure — make sure you understand how ownership is transferred over time.

09

Legal Process and Completion

Your solicitor will handle the legal transfer of ownership. For Islamic mortgages, this may involve slightly more complex legal documentation than a conventional mortgage. Budget for additional legal fees to reflect this. Completion typically happens 4–12 weeks after receiving your formal offer.

Documents You'll Need

Identity & Residency

  • Passport or national ID
  • Proof of address (utility bill, bank statement)
  • Visa/residency documents (if applicable)

Income (Employed)

  • Last 3 months' payslips
  • Last 2 years' P60s
  • Employment contract (new jobs)
  • 3 months' bank statements

Income (Self-Employed)

  • Last 2–3 years' SA302s
  • Tax year overviews
  • Accountant's reference
  • 6 months' business bank statements

Property & Deposit

  • Memorandum of sale
  • Proof of deposit source
  • Gift letter (if gifted deposit)
  • Solicitor details

Tips to Maximise Your Approval Chances

  • Avoid applying for other credit in the 6 months before your application — multiple hard searches can lower your score.
  • Reduce your credit card balances — aim to use less than 30% of your available credit limit.
  • Ensure your deposit is clearly documented — providers need to see that your deposit is genuine savings, not an undisclosed loan.
  • Be consistent on all forms — inconsistencies between documents raise red flags and slow down applications.
  • Use a specialist Islamic mortgage broker — they know which providers are most likely to approve your specific circumstances.
